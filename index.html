  • FORGE: Feasible Online Reduced-Order Reference Generation and Whole-Body Execution for Agile Humanoid Locomotion
    arXiv:2610.37974v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: This paper presents a two-stage learning framework for versatile and agile humanoid locomotion that couples online reduced-order model (ROM) reference generation with whole-body tracking control. In the first stage, we train a command-conditioned whole-body reference generator using single rigid body dynamics combined with full humanoid kinematics. In the second stage, the frozen ROM policy is rolled out online to generate references for training a whole-body tracking policy on the full-order dynamics model. Learning a kino-dynamically consistent reference generator in the simplified ROM space enables us to generate references over a broad command space and over multiple locomotion strategies using a single unified framework. We demonstrate the approach on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, achieving longitudinal velocities from -2.0 m/s to 2.8 m/s, lateral velocities up to +-1.0 m/s, angular velocities up to +-3.0 rad/s, one-legged hopping, and continuous transitions across the commands, all using one unified reference policy and one whole-body tracking policy.

  • METATRON: A Promptable Whole-Body Execution Layer for Humanoids
    arXiv:2610.38027v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Humanoid controllers often require task-specific training despite sharing the need for balance and whole-body coordination. We present Metatron, a promptable whole-body execution layer combining a generative base policy with a programmable skill layer. The base policy receives partial goals, collectively called a prompt, that can combine timed body-part targets, velocity commands, and style cues. It produces diverse whole-body motions consistent with these goals. Training combines motion capture, synthetic recovery trajectories, and retargeted scene-interaction motions. After training, the policy is frozen. Stateful programs called skills express task intent as prompts at each control step, drawing rough geometric sketches that the policy turns into coordinated movement. Skills are ordinary code, refined by coding agents that analyze motion data and fit compact rules without task-specific policy training. On the physical Unitree G1, one frozen checkpoint supports walking, recovery, teleoperated object pickup, and sitting on furniture. Comparative evaluations show higher reaching success and push robustness than the tested reference-tracking pipelines. The same recipe produces climbing and acrobatics on a simulated character. Metatron enables coding agents to turn learned motor capabilities into new skills through code, without further policy training.

    arXiv:2610.37799v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Human-level dexterity remains a central open problem in robotics, yet few simulation frameworks cover the full workflow of dexterous manipulation, from setting up a robot to collecting dexterous demonstrations and evaluating policies.   • An Empirical Study of Wrist-Target Construction and Joint-Angle Limits in Humanoid Arm Retargeting
    arXiv:2610.37801v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Motion retargeting maps a demonstrated human movement onto a robot to compute its joint motion. This study examines how the construction of the desired wrist position affects whether computed arm joint angles reach their permitted limits on a Unitree H1-2. A reconstructed target is formed from demonstrated arm directions and robot segment lengths, so its shoulder-to-wrist distance depends on the demonstrated angle between the segments even when no posture-matching preference is applied. A reach-normalized target retains that direction while rescaling the distance, using the same processed demonstrations and the same joint-angle method. Across a forward-reach sweep the reconstructed target places a joint angle at a bound in 10 of 20 repetitions and the reach-normalized target in none. Across 25 recordings from one participant the corresponding counts are 8 and 6 on the reaching arm and 14 and 5 on the supporting arm, and a second participant repeats the bricklaying and lifting comparison. In hardware replay a joint is commanded to a bound in 12 of 16 direction-matching arm-records and in none of the 16 normalized records, and within the eligible records both tracking error and executed torque at those frames exceed their values elsewhere in the same run. Cross-body reach and arm-to-arm clearance identify limits of radial adjustment. The findings support treating target construction and posture matching as separate retargeting decisions, and evaluating both alongside the wrist positions and clearance that manipulation requires.

  • TACOS: Terrain-Aware COntact Supervision for Visual Footstep Planning
    arXiv:2610.37806v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: Humans navigate their environment based on their vision system, anticipating how the ground will respond before committing to a step. Self-supervised learning could provide legged robots the same ability, but the supervisory signal is typically chosen on intuition, and its effect on what the model learns is rarely examined. We present Terrain-Aware COntact Supervision (TACOS), which proposes a meaningful and consistent footstep instability score, based on the residual of a factor graph in legged state estimation. It projects the continuous score into preceding images to supervise a dense visual predictor. We propose two variants, a hard score that exploits hindsight and a causal soft score available immediately. Both metrics are compared against state-of-the-art alternatives in two independent evaluations that share no data or labels. On 1716 manually annotated contacts, both variants outperform every evaluated alternative at detecting instability and ranking its severity, improving pooled severity ranking by 28 % over the strongest baseline. On three outdoor test runs, our metric also trains the most accurate visual predictor, improving rank correlation by 58 % over the strongest alternative, and by a further 25 % when supplemented with onboard context. In a real-world experiment over a 50 m forest trajectory, the trained predictor handles non-geometric terrain properties such as mud and vegetation, while stepping around geometric obstacles like fallen branches. Code is available for reproducibility at https://github.com/norlab-ulaval/TACOS.
